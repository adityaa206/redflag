Editor’s note
What this scan tells us

The target’s perimeter carries two findings that, on their own, would each justify pausing the integration plan. The first is a Microsoft SMB remote-code-execution vulnerability that appears on CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list — not theoretical, but weaponised in the wild today. The second is an internet-facing Remote Desktop pathway, the most common ransomware ingress vector observed across enterprise breaches in the past 36 months.

“Operate the acquired business standalone, behind a clean-room boundary, until the pre-connection blockers are remediated or formally mitigated.”

Neither finding requires a clever attack chain — both are reachable from the public internet with off-the-shelf tooling. Connecting these systems to the parent network on Day 1 would extend that exposure to the acquirer’s estate without remediation in place. The recommended posture, accordingly, is isolation: VDI-brokered access where the business case demands it, and no network or directory link to the parent until the perimeter has been verified clean.
